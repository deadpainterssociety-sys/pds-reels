Patterns (n = 5):
- Length 9.5–37 s (median 20 s). Ours: 61–68 s.
- 4 of 5 have no voice-over; the story is one sentence of burned-in text per image. 5 of 5 carry it in captions.
- Frame 1 states the WHOLE premise as a plain claim over the strongest image (5/5). No teaser question, no date, no
  "wait for it". The viewer knows at once what they are watching → VVSA.
- A cut every ~1.5–4 s; the images ESCALATE (each stronger than the last: Charnley's portraits more distorted; Elizabeth's
  face worse; Rockwell's neighbour messier) → AVD: people stay to see the next step.
- Payoff = the human cost or a twist ("…took his own life at 41"; "the experiment revealed how far people could go"),
  or a reflective question to the viewer ("If nobody was watching, would you still care?"). Then it loops.
- Themes are universal, not art-insider: madness, cruelty, beauty standards, pregnancy and shame, class. Small channels
  (10–17k subs) — the reach comes from the theme, not from the audience they had.
- Titles = the premise as a statement: "The Painter Who…", "This Painting Shows…", "The history of…".

→ FORMULAS.md F3 (Caption Short) and F4 (The Series) were written from this; "Then vs now" and "Meme dialogue" are
candidates.

Per Short:
- **Rédemption** — premise: the elegant woman in white at a society dinner is not a woman but a spirit nobody at the
  table can see. Frame 1 = the full painting + a question that challenges what you see. Cuts every ~4 s, ALL inside the one
  painting: "Look at her left hand" → "That's not a human hand" → "Behind her… Christ on the cross" → "In her hand, an
  iris… the symbol of faith" → pan to the diners: "The room is full of people, but lost in their own pleasures" → "None of
  them can see her" → "because she is…" → one-word card **REDEMPTION** over her face. Ends "Meaning of the painting in the
  comments". Why: VVSA from a frame-1 dare about the image on screen; AVD from a chain of "look at …" clues that build to
  a single-word reveal; the comment hand-off adds comments/shares.
- **Mona Lisa theft** — our F1 shape almost exactly (premise as the first line, 1911 theft, Perugia, Picasso questioned,
  2 years, Florence, "a crime made the Mona Lisa the most famous painting on Earth"), voiced, 66 s, AI-cartoon images,
  ends on "What mystery should Curio Kids solve next?". The ratio is inflated by a 447-view median, but 1.3M views at 66 s
  says the length is not the problem when line 1 states the whole premise.
- **St Agnes** — premise: a 13-year-old refused a Roman governor's son, was stripped and jailed, and her hair grew to cover
  her. One painting, slow pushes on details (face, hands, the angel with the white cloth), one sentence per card, each
  harsher than the last; ends on a cliffhanger ("But what the Romans did next… was unforgivable") → "Read the full story
  in the Comments." Why: a child + cruelty + a miracle (universal theme), the escalation holds AVD.
- **Asia vs Europe** — a comparison meme: 7 Chinese-style beauties (several modern illustrations, not medieval), then
  "Medieval European Women": Massys's Ugly Duchess, a Pisanello profile, Isabella-of-Castile-style portraits. No story,
  no voice; the contrast is the joke and the comments argue (2k). Not our genre, but frame 1 announces the format.
- **Naples hinge** — 6 s, reposted phone footage (watermark of another account): the altar canvas swings open like a door
  and a 1645 fresco appears. All facts in a paragraph above the video. Pure loop: the reveal is too fast, so people
  rewatch. Reposting others' footage = not for us.
- **Jesse Speaks** — fringe "UFOs in Renaissance paintings" reaction, 159 s, creator on camera under the painting. A
  big channel's mid-size hit; the claim is not true storytelling. Counter-example: long Shorts can work when frame 1 makes
  a bold claim and a face talks to you, but we won't trade truth for it.

Patterns (n = 11, both safaris):
- Length 6–159 s, median 25 s; 8 of 11 ≤ 37 s. The two longer ones that broke out (52 s, 66 s) both state the premise in
  line 1 and keep a new clue or event every ~4 s.
- Frame 1 states the premise or dares the viewer about the image on screen: 11/11. Phrased as a question: 3/11
  (Elizabeth, Rédemption, "Did you know…") — all questions about the thing on screen, never a teaser.
- NEW — **one painting, detail by detail**: 2/11, and they are the two highest ratios of this week (1047×, 296×; same
  channel, 41.8k subs). The camera never leaves the painting; each card points at a detail ("Look at her left hand",
  "Behind her…", "In her hand, an iris…"); the reveal is one word on a card.
- Escalation to a payoff: 9/11 (not the Asia/Europe meme or the Naples loop).
- Ending: payoff sent to the comments 2/11 (Stories Behind Art), a question to the viewer 3/11, a loop 3/11.
- No voice-over (music or silence + burned-in text): at least 6/11; voice confirmed 3/11 (Mona Lisa, Jesse, Nyxie on camera).
- Themes: cruelty to a woman or child, madness, beauty standards, the sacred-strange (a ghost, a miracle) — universal.

Not for us (noted so we don't drift): AI-cartoon reconstructions, reposted footage, withholding the payoff in the
comments (our rule: the video answers its cover question; an unanswered story risks satisfaction and "misleading"
reports), fringe claims.

### Proposals for the lab

**ONE change: F3 gets a "Look closer" variant (one painting, detail by detail).** For stories whose payoff is inside the
painting (`hidden`, `sacred-strange`), an F3 may stay on the main image for the whole reel: card 1 a dare or claim about
the figure on screen (≤ 12 words: "This woman at the dinner party isn't a woman."), then 3–5 "Look at …" detail cards, each
a tighter close-up than the last (hand → background → object), a short one-word or two-word reveal card in the highlight
colour, then the plain ending. 28–52 s; the video answers the question itself (no "meaning in the comments").
Evidence: Stories Behind Art · "Rédemption" (J. L. Stewart) · 6.0M · 1047× · 52 s; Stories Behind Art · "St Agnes in
Prison" (F. C. Cowper) · 1.7M · 296× · 28 s; it also matches F2's "Look at …" beats, at a third of the length.
