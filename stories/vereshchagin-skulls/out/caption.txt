=== INSTAGRAM (Reel) ===
This pile of skulls is a gift.

Vasily Vereshchagin painted The Apotheosis of War in 1871, and he had seen war up close. Three years earlier he was with the Russian army at the siege of Samarkand, in Central Asia, and was decorated with the Cross of St George for bravery.

Then he painted what war leaves behind: a dead plain, a ruined city, crows, and a pyramid of skulls. Russia banned it from public exhibition, and in Germany Field Marshal Moltke reportedly kept his soldiers away from it.

In 1904 Vereshchagin went to war one last time, aboard the battleship Petropavlovsk. On 13 April she hit mines off Port Arthur and sank; he died with nearly 700 others, among them Admiral Makarov.

He had first called the painting The Triumph of Tamerlane. Then he wrote on the frame: "Dedicated to all the great conquerors: past, present and future."

It hangs in the Tretyakov Gallery, in Moscow.
