=== INSTAGRAM (Reel) ===
This Goya was stolen to pay for TV licences.

Goya painted the Duke of Wellington in Madrid in 1812. In 1961 the National Gallery paid £140,000 to keep it from going to an American collector, and on 21 August 1961, less than three weeks after it went on show, it vanished.

Ransom notes followed: the thief wanted £140,000 given to charity to buy TV licences for poor pensioners. For almost four years nobody found it. In May 1965 it turned up in the left-luggage office at Birmingham New Street station. That July, Kempton Bunton, a 61-year-old retired driver from Newcastle, turned himself in.

The jury cleared him of stealing the painting. He got three months for the frame, which was never returned. Then in 2012, released files showed that his son John had confessed in 1969 to being the real thief.

P.S. While it was missing, a copy turned up in Dr No's lair in the first James Bond film.
