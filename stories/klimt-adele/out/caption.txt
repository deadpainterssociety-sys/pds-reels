The Nazis stole this portrait, and erased her name.

In 1907 Gustav Klimt finished a portrait of Adele Bloch-Bauer in oil, silver and gold, paid for by her husband Ferdinand, a Jewish sugar magnate. In 1938 the Nazis took Austria. Ferdinand fled, and his Klimt paintings were seized.

The portrait ended up in Vienna's state gallery under a new title: "Lady in Gold" (Dame in Gold). No name, nothing to say the woman in it was Jewish.

Her niece, Maria Altmann, had escaped to America. In 2000, at 84, she sued Austria; the case went all the way to the US Supreme Court. In 2006 an Austrian arbitration panel ruled for her, and the paintings came back to the family. That June, Ronald Lauder bought the portrait for $135 million, a record at the time, and it got its name back: Adele Bloch-Bauer I. It hangs in the Neue Galerie, on Fifth Avenue in New York.
