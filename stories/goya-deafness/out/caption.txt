Watch Goya's world go dark.

In 1777 he paints sunny picnics and parasols, cartoons for the royal tapestries of El Pardo. Then, between late 1792 and early 1793, an illness nobody has ever diagnosed leaves him deaf, at 46.

In 1794 he paints a courtyard of madmen wrestling while a keeper beats them, "a scene I witnessed at Zaragoza", he writes to a friend. In 1814 come French soldiers shooting Spaniards before dawn (The Third of May 1808). Around 1820, in his seventies, he covers the walls of his own house outside Madrid with the Black Paintings: witches, a goat-devil, and this dog, sinking behind a slope.

In 1824 he leaves Spain for Bordeaux. There, at about 80, he draws an old man with a white beard on two sticks, and above him writes "Aún aprendo": I am still learning. He died on 16 April 1828, aged 82.

The Dog, the Black Paintings, the Parasol and the drawing are all in the Prado, in Madrid.
