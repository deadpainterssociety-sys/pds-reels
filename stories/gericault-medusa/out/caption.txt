On 2 July 1816 the French frigate Méduse ran aground off West Africa. There was room in the boats for about 250 of the 400 on board, so around 150 people were piled onto a raft. The boats were meant to tow it. After a few miles, they let it go.

For 13 days the raft drifted. The men fought, killed the weakest and ate the dead. When the ship Argus found them, only 15 were still alive.

In 1818 the young painter Théodore Géricault met two of the survivors and built a scale model of the raft with them. Then he sketched bodies at the morgue of the Hôpital Beaujon and took severed limbs back to his studio to paint them as they decayed. One account says he kept a severed head on his studio roof for two weeks.

At the 1819 Salon the critics were split: some were gripped, others called it a "pile of corpses". Géricault died in 1824, aged 32, and the Louvre bought the painting that year.
