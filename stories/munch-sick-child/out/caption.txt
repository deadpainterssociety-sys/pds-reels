Watch Edvard Munch paint his sister's death, six times over.

In 1877 his sister Sophie died of tuberculosis. She was 15. Eight years later he painted the scene: Sophie in bed, a woman bowed beside her. He painted it, scrubbed it out and painted it again for more than a year, and showed it in 1886 under the title 'Study'. The critic Andreas Aubert called it a 'half-rubbed-out sketch'.

In 1896, in Paris, he painted it again for a collector (Gothenburg). In 1907, twice more (Thiel Gallery, Stockholm; Tate, London). By 1925, nearly fifty years after she died, another one (Munch Museum, Oslo), and a last one dated 1926.

Why? He felt the first was too grey, 'heavy as lead'. The Munch Museum thinks no single picture could hold the whole memory. Yet that grey first version is the one he called his breakthrough.
