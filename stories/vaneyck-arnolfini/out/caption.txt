Look in the mirror. Someone is watching this couple.

Bruges, 1434. Jan van Eyck painted an Italian merchant, probably Giovanni di Nicolao Arnolfini, and his wife. On the back wall hangs a round convex mirror; around its frame, ten tiny painted scenes from the Passion of Christ.

In the glass: the whole room, bent, and two men in the doorway. One in blue, one in red. Above the mirror, in big letters on the wall, van Eyck signed in Latin: "Johannes de eyck fuit hic 1434" – Jan van Eyck was here. So the man in red is probably the painter himself.

In 1934 the historian Erwin Panofsky argued that the two men were witnesses and the painting a record of a marriage. Many historians now doubt it. But for almost 600 years, the painter has stood in that doorway, watching us watch them. It hangs in the National Gallery, London.

P.S. She isn't pregnant: she's holding up a bulky, fashionable gown.
