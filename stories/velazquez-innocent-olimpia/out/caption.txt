Velázquez painted Pope Innocent X in Rome in 1650. But Rome said the man in the red chair answered to a woman: his brother's widow, Olimpia Maidalchini.

A Venetian ambassador wrote in 1645 that she had "great influence with him". Another account (1652) said that when posts fell vacant, nothing was decided without her. Romans called her the papessa, the lady pope. Velázquez painted her too; that portrait vanished after 1724 and resurfaced at Sotheby's in 2019.

Then the darkest story: when the Pope died in January 1655, she supposedly refused to pay for his coffin, and his body lay abandoned for three days. But the Vatican's ceremony diaries record his body lying in state and his coffin sealed before the cardinals. Historians trace the tale partly to the scandal writer Gregorio Leti.

Her power was real. Her cruelest legend probably wasn't.
