This was one of Hitler's favorite paintings. But it began with a widow.

Florence, 1880. Marie Berna, whose husband had died years before, visited Arnold Böcklin's studio and asked for "a picture to dream on". He painted her a rocky island with dark cypress trees and tombs cut into the cliffs, and, at her request, a coffin in a boat and a figure in white.

Böcklin painted it five times. His dealer, Fritz Gurlitt, gave it the name that stuck: Isle of the Dead. Prints spread across Germany; Rachmaninoff wrote his symphonic poem after seeing a black-and-white copy.

In the 1930s Adolf Hitler bought the third version (1883). It hung at the Berghof, his mountain home, and from 1940 in his new Reich Chancellery in Berlin.

A picture made to mourn one man, watching over the man who sent millions to their deaths. It hangs in the Alte Nationalgalerie in Berlin.
