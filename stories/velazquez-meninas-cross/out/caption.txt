That red cross on Velázquez's chest wasn't there when he finished Las Meninas.

He painted it in 1656 and put himself in the picture, brush in hand. The cross is the badge of the Order of Santiago, a knighthood for nobles only: all four grandparents had to be noble and "Old Christians", and no tradesmen were allowed. Selling paintings counted.

So the Order investigated him. Almost 150 people were questioned, and his file claimed he never charged for his art, he only painted to please King Philip IV. He still couldn't prove noble blood, so the king asked Pope Alexander VII for a dispensation. On 28 November 1659 Velázquez finally became a knight, three years after the painting was finished. Nine months later he was dead.

So who painted the cross? His early biographer Antonio Palomino wrote that the king had it added after Velázquez's death, "and some say that his Majesty himself painted it." Others think Velázquez added it himself. Nobody knows for sure.
